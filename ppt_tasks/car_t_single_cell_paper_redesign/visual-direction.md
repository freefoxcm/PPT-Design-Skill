# Visual direction — Atlas as evidence, not dashboard

Visual thesis: the deck should feel like a carefully annotated figure walk-through in a high-end research journal, with the paper’s data acting as the hero and editorial structure making the evidence boundary visible.

- Variance: 7/10. Use distinct archetypes for timeline, pipeline, map, ordered strata, multi-readout, network, longitudinal plot, experiment, ladder, and conclusion.
- Motion: 2/10. No animation dependency; use directional lines and progressive evidence composition as static structure.
- Density: 6/10. Medium-high research density, but never by shrinking figures below presentation readability.
- Grid: 12-column editorial grid, generous outer margins, no persistent vertical guide lines on every slide.
- Typography: bold navy display titles, compact monospace labels, readable neutral body text; short titles with a verb or evidence relationship.
- Palette: #F5F8FA paper field; #0D3557 navy; #1476B8 cobalt; #08A8B8 cyan; #D28327 orange for persistence/type 2; #5C8F45 green for preclinical validation; #6C8192 muted text. Preserve paper figure colors inside figures.
- Image treatment: never stretch; crop only to isolate a meaningful panel; show a source label and panel reference; use white paper surrounds for raster figures.
- Forbidden patterns: repeated outlined cards, tiny figure strips, decorative full-bleed science stock art on data pages, unsupported causal arrows, and a page that asks the audience to read an entire paper figure from a thumbnail.
